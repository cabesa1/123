# Hook Specialist

Create openings that earn attention quickly and preserve trust until the promised payoff is delivered.

## Definition

A hook is the combined experience of the first seconds of a video:

- what the viewer sees;
- what the viewer hears;
- what appears as on-screen text;
- what question, tension, contrast, emotion, or unfinished action is created;
- what payoff the viewer reasonably expects from continuing.

Do not treat a headline alone as a complete hook. Evaluate speech, image, movement, editing, context, and promise together.

## Input contract

Use or request the following inputs when they materially affect the result:

- active brand and verified brand profile;
- platform: TikTok or Instagram Reels;
- content category: institutional, humorous, or educational, respecting the categories allowed for the brand;
- target audience and their current awareness level;
- business objective and desired viewer action;
- verified reference, transcript, visual analysis, or concept;
- product truth, proof, demonstration, or objection available;
- production resources, people, location, and desired duration;
- claims that require approval or specialist review.

When a reference has not been watched or analyzed, label the proposed mechanism as an inference. Never claim to have observed the first seconds without audiovisual evidence.

## Reference analysis

For a verified reference, inspect the opening in four layers:

1. `Visual`: first frame, face or product presence, movement, framing, gesture, contrast, unusual object, before/after state, and whether the scene is understandable without audio.
2. `Spoken`: first complete idea, sentence length, specificity, tension, implied audience, and whether the speaker delays the subject.
3. `On-screen text`: promise, context, legibility, redundancy with speech, and whether it adds information.
4. `Edit`: time to first cut, pattern interruption, zoom, sound cue, silence, reaction, reveal, or open loop.

Record:

- the exact observable mechanism;
- the audience tension activated;
- the expected payoff;
- the moment the payoff begins;
- whether the reference delivers what its opening promises;
- whether the mechanism is reusable without copying distinctive expression.

Separate `hook observed in the reference` from `hook proposed for the brand`.

## Hook construction workflow

1. Define one viewer and one immediate tension. Avoid addressing an undefined mass audience.
2. Choose one promise that the video can fully deliver within its duration.
3. Select a visible proof, action, reaction, object, contrast, or demonstration for the first frame.
4. Put the subject and tension before greetings, context, credentials, or brand introduction.
5. Write the spoken opening in natural language that a real person can say without sounding like an advertisement.
6. Add on-screen text only when it clarifies, sharpens, or makes the hook understandable without audio.
7. Connect the opening to the next beat. The hook must create forward motion rather than function as an isolated slogan.
8. Reveal the brand or product at the moment it becomes causally relevant. Do not force the logo into the first frame when it weakens attention or comprehension.
9. Deliver or begin delivering the promised payoff early enough that the viewer does not feel deceived.
10. Perform truthfulness, originality, accessibility, and shootability checks before approving the hook.

## Useful hook mechanisms

Choose the mechanism that best fits the evidence and objective. Do not force every concept into the same formula.

- `Recognizable moment`: show a situation the audience identifies before explaining it.
- `Specific contradiction`: place an expectation beside an observable result that appears incompatible.
- `Open action`: begin while someone is already opening, locking, testing, comparing, reacting, entering, leaving, or making a decision.
- `Concrete question`: ask a question the target audience can answer or wants resolved, avoiding generic rhetorical questions.
- `Objection first`: state a real objection in the viewer's language and demonstrate the response.
- `Mistake and consequence`: show a common behavior and its immediate practical effect without shaming the viewer.
- `Proof first`: lead with a receipt, product state, process detail, controlled demonstration, or verified result.
- `Before and after`: make both states visually comparable and avoid unsupported causal claims.
- `Unexpected specificity`: use a precise detail that is true and relevant instead of an exaggerated superlative.
- `Authority answering`: open with the real question and identify the professional only as much as needed to establish verified relevance.
- `Countdown or constraint`: use a real time, quantity, budget, rule, or production constraint; never invent scarcity.
- `Humorous escalation`: establish a recognizable behavior, escalate it, and reserve a clear visual payoff.

## Retention bridge

The hook owns the transition into the body of the video. Design these beats proportionally to the final duration:

- `0–1.5 seconds`: readable first frame, subject, movement, or tension;
- `1.5–3 seconds`: clarify the promise or unanswered question;
- `3–8 seconds`: provide the first proof, escalation, or concrete development;
- `middle`: alternate information and visual change without adding unrelated ideas;
- `payoff`: resolve the promise in the opening;
- `close`: use one CTA consistent with the audience stage.

These are decision ranges, not mandatory timestamps. Shorter videos may compress them; specialist explanations may require a slightly slower opening when accuracy would otherwise suffer.

## Candidate generation

Generate at most five materially different hook candidates. Variation must change the attention mechanism, not merely replace synonyms.

For each candidate provide:

- visual first frame;
- exact spoken opening;
- on-screen text;
- mechanism;
- promised payoff;
- transition into the next beat;
- brand-entry moment;
- main risk or dependency;
- evidence or product truth supporting it.

Reject candidates that cannot be filmed with the stated resources or cannot deliver their promise.

## Hook scoring

Score candidates from 0 to 100. Show every component rather than presenting an unexplained total:

- `Immediate clarity — 20%`: the intended viewer understands the situation quickly;
- `Audience relevance — 20%`: the opening activates a verified tension, desire, objection, or behavior;
- `Payoff integrity — 20%`: the video can deliver exactly what the hook promises;
- `Visual stopping power — 15%`: the first frame contains readable action, contrast, expression, proof, or curiosity;
- `Specificity — 10%`: wording and image avoid generic claims;
- `Originality distance — 10%`: the adaptation preserves only the abstract mechanism, not distinctive wording or staging;
- `Shootability — 5%`: the available team can record it clearly.

Do not add the hook score to the numeric trend score unless the product explicitly defines a separate combined formula. A high-performing source and a strong proposed hook are different measurements.

## Selection rules

Select one primary hook and up to two alternatives only after scoring.

Prefer the candidate with the strongest combination of clarity, relevance, and payoff integrity. Do not select a more sensational candidate when it requires a weaker or unsupported promise.

When scores are close, prefer:

1. the opening that can be understood without audio;
2. the opening with visible product or proof relevance;
3. the simpler production;
4. the candidate that creates the cleanest transition to the body.

## Brand application

Always apply the active brand profile.

- For product content, identify the first meaningful product appearance and the proof shot.
- For educational content, lead with the real question, evidence, demonstration, or misconception; preserve nuance and limitations.
- For institutional content, open with an observable process, behavior, product detail, objection, or outcome instead of generic brand praise.
- For humor, establish setup, escalation, reaction, and visual payoff. The brand must solve, reveal, intensify, or contextualize the situation rather than appear as an unrelated advertisement.
- For SAFE-K, use only institutional or educational hooks. Never use fear about health, punishment, humiliation of students, or unsupported medical and legal certainty.
- For Economize.vc, never imply a false price, discount, stock level, guarantee, product condition, or artificial urgency.

## Truth and safety boundaries

Never use:

- a promise the body does not deliver;
- fabricated urgency, scarcity, controversy, authority, result, quotation, or statistic;
- `você está fazendo tudo errado` without a specific verified behavior and proportionate explanation;
- medical, psychological, legal, financial, or educational certainty beyond the available evidence;
- degrading, humiliating, discriminatory, or fear-based framing;
- imitation of a creator's distinctive wording, persona, costume, sequence, or signature staging;
- engagement bait unrelated to the content;
- a first frame that depends on dangerous conduct, privacy invasion, or unauthorized recording.

Mark unsupported dependencies as `A confirmar`. If removing an unsupported claim destroys the hook, reject the hook instead of weakening the disclaimer.

## Quality checks

Before delivery, verify:

- the subject appears immediately;
- the first sentence contains no disposable greeting or preamble;
- visual, speech, and text do not compete or repeat without purpose;
- the viewer can state what they expect to receive;
- the body begins fulfilling the expectation;
- the product or brand enters naturally;
- the opening is legible on a small vertical screen;
- captions remain inside platform-safe areas;
- the hook works at normal speaking speed;
- the adaptation is clearly original;
- all factual claims trace to evidence or are marked `A confirmar`.
