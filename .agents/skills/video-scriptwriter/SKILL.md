# Video Scriptwriter

Create an original, shootable script that fits the chosen platform, duration, audience, and production capacity.

## Input contract

Use the approved concept, objective, audience, platform, duration, tone, speaker, offer, CTA, proof available, and production constraints. If proof is missing, do not fabricate it; insert a clearly labeled `A confirmar` field.

## Script workflow

1. Preserve the concept's strategic promise and source evidence, but do not copy the reference's wording or distinctive execution.
2. Write three hook options and choose one with a short rationale.
3. Allocate time by scene. Give every scene a start and end time, camera framing, physical action, exact spoken line, on-screen text, edit or sound cue, prop, and purpose. Ensure the total fits the requested duration at a natural speaking pace.
4. Open with the payoff, tension, or problem; remove greetings and slow setup unless intentionally required.
5. Use concrete visuals. Every spoken claim should have a matching shot, screen recording, prop, caption, or proof element.
6. End with one CTA appropriate to the funnel stage.
7. Perform a shootability pass: a person receiving the script must be able to film it without asking what to say, where to stand, what to show, or how to cut the scene.
8. Perform a continuity pass across wardrobe, props, screen direction, product state, lighting, and audio. Flag every dependency that must be prepared before recording.
9. Perform a claim-safety pass. Replace unsupported promises with demonstrations, opinions, or fields marked `A confirmar`.

## Applicability requirements

Adapt the production plan to the resources actually available. Never default to a generic four-step outline.

- For solo production, specify tripod position, self-recording action, and cuts that one person can perform.
- For a team, assign each role and identify who appears, films, speaks, and handles props.
- For product videos, specify when the product first appears, its condition, the proof shot, and the final brand frame.
- For humor, define setup, escalation, visual payoff, reaction beat, and the exact moment the brand becomes relevant.
- For educational content, define the question, demonstration or evidence, explanation, practical takeaway, and limitation.
- For institutional content, replace vague brand praise with a visible behavior, process, product detail, or customer outcome that can be filmed.
- If the requested execution exceeds available resources, provide a simpler alternative that preserves the hook and payoff.

## Output contract

Provide:

- platform, target duration, format, difficulty, and required resources;
- chosen hook plus two alternatives;
- a table with time window, shot, spoken line, on-screen text, edit or sound note, and purpose;
- named people or roles, location, wardrobe, props, product state, camera orientation, lighting, and audio setup;
- caption, CTA, cover text, and three title variants;
- filming checklist and editing checklist;
- continuity checklist, claims requiring approval, and a final pre-publication verification list;
- claims or assets marked `A confirmar`;
- a simpler version when the requested production is medium or difficult.

Do not claim exact spoken duration unless measured. Estimate conservatively and recommend a read-through before recording.
